# SONIC PULSE — VENUE CONTRACT CONTEXT

Brief for the contracts session drafting the **venue agreement**. Written
13 Aug 2026; the event is **43 days out and the venue is still not booked** —
this is the critical path, and every other workstream (permits, security,
decor, installation placement, the address email to ticket holders) is
blocked behind it.

Companion files: `EVENT_CONTEXT.md` (physical event in detail),
`FINANCE_CONTEXT.md` (money, KNOWN vs OPEN), `OPS_CONTEXT.md` (workstreams
and timeline). Currency BDT (৳); the owner sometimes writes 1,00,000 for
100,000. A separate contract already exists with the **art-installation and
Main Stage vendor** — this venue contract must not contradict its 20 Sep
delivery deadline (see §5).

Facts below are sourced from the festival's locked data and published site
(sonicpulsefestival.com). Anything marked **OPEN** has no decision yet —
never invent a value; raise it as a question for the owner.

## 1. The event, as the venue must understand it

- **Sonic Pulse**, organised by **Dhaka Music Festival** (@dhakamusicfestival).
- **Friday 25 September 2026, 4:00 PM → Saturday 26 September, 9:30 AM** —
  **17.5 continuous hours**, overnight, single night, no second day.
- **800+ attendees**, **all ages** (the strict 18+ rule was removed 13 Aug 2026), ID-verified at entry (NID, passport or birth certificate).
- Two stages running **in sequence, never simultaneously**: Main Stage
  4:00 PM – 4:30 AM, Sunrise Stage 4:30 – 9:30 AM.
- Nine large-scale art installations, nine activity zones, a night market,
  a food quarter, and a midnight fire ceremony.
- Primarily **outdoors with covered areas**; the event **proceeds in light
  rain** (published policy) — so the site must remain safe and operable wet.

## 3. Structural, load and safety demands to disclose in writing

Disclose these to the venue **before signing** — several exceed what a
routine event contract contemplates, and silent non-disclosure is how
permission gets revoked mid-build.

- **Main Stage: seven pillars, tallest approximately 40 feet**, built to an
  agreed blueprint, carrying production sound and lighting rigging loads.
  Requires ground bearing capacity, overhead clearance, and no
  power-line/tree conflict.
- **Two climbable public structures** — CHROMA (lit staircase spiralling up
  to a crown platform) and THE EMPTY THRONE (guests stand on the seat; the
  grounds' highest guest vantage point). Public foot traffic at height, all
  night, in the dark.
- **Overhead suspension** over standing crowds (Mycelia canopies, Glowtide
  jellyfish avenue).
- **Guest-loaded netting** raised off the ground (Cloud Nine, nine bays).
- **Open flame in four forms**: Ember Rites fire performers; the Empty
  Throne's twin flame torches burning through the night; Feast Quarter
  open-flame cooking; and **THE GREAT BURN — a monumental bamboo-and-woven-
  light kite (ICARUS) deliberately set alight at midnight**, with a crowd
  gathered behind an ember line. This is the single highest-risk element of
  the event and needs explicit, written venue consent plus fire-authority
  clearance.
- **Water activities**: boats carrying 2–4 guests on the lake through the
  night; a pool in use including at dawn. Drowning risk, lifeguard and
  boat-safety obligations.
- **Continuous overnight amplified sound to 9:30 AM** — noise tolerance,
  neighbour proximity and any curfew must be settled explicitly, not
  assumed.

## 4. Services and infrastructure — settle who supplies each

For every line, the contract should state **venue-supplied, organiser-
supplied, or organiser-supplied-with-venue-permission**, and who bears cost:

- **Power**: 17.5 hours continuous for two stages, UV rigs, nine lit
  installations and all activity lighting. Generators, distribution,
  redundancy and overnight fuel. Metering and utility charges. (No power
  vendor appointed yet — **OPEN**.)
- **Water**: drinking-water points for 800+ overnight; supply for pool and
  sanitation.
- **Sanitation**: toilets sized for 800+ across 17.5 hours, overnight
  servicing, waste removal, morning-after clearance. (No vendor — **OPEN**.)
- **Parking**: published FAQ commits to "limited parking on-site" plus a
  clearly marked **drop-off/pick-up zone** — the venue must actually
  support both, including overnight and a 9:30 AM departure surge.
- **Accessibility**: published FAQ commits to **wheelchair-accessible
  pathways and a dedicated viewing area near both stages** — a contractual
  promise to attendees that the site must physically allow.
- **Chill-out / quiet zone** away from the sound systems — also published.
- **Covered areas** for light rain, per published policy.
- **Medical point**, security posts, staff welfare areas, storage,
  artist/green-room space.
- **Site map**: the FAQ promises attendees a site map in their ticket email
  — it cannot be produced until the venue is locked.

## 5. Dates, load-in and access window

**Owner confirmation (13 Aug 2026): the installations are fabricated
OFF SITE and transported to the venue on the day of the festival.** This
supersedes the earlier assumption that they were built on site, and it
changes what the venue contract must secure — the risk moves from a long
build occupancy to a single, unforgiving load-in day.

- **20 September 2026** — the installation vendor's contractual deadline.
  Given off-site fabrication, this now means **fabrication complete and
  inspected at the workshop**, not standing at the venue. The installation
  contract should say so explicitly; "delivered by 20 Sep" read literally
  against a day-of transport plan is ambiguous and worth correcting there.
- **25 September, load-in** — six monumental installations transported and
  erected **before 4:00 PM gates**, alongside sound, lighting and power
  build. This makes the following venue terms critical, not routine:
  - **Vehicle access** onto the grounds for large trucks, with route,
    surface and turning capacity for 12 ft+ pieces.
  - **Gate, route and overhead clearance** — the pieces are monumental;
    a venue whose only entrance is a narrow arch or has low cabling can
    physically refuse the build.
  - **Crane / lifting equipment access and standing space**, including for
    overhead rigging in the grove (Mycelia) and the tall pieces.
  - **A load-in window long enough to be survivable** — start time on
    25 Sep, and whether earlier access on 23–24 Sep is available as
    contingency. There is **no slack before gates**: a transport delay has
    nowhere to go.
- **Main Stage — OPEN and urgent.** Seven pillars, tallest ~40 ft. Whether
  this is also day-of assembly or needs earlier on-site days has not been
  confirmed with the vendor. Ask before signing: if the stage needs build
  days, the venue access window must cover them.
- **Event day**: 25 September, gates 4:00 PM.
- **Strike**: from 9:30 AM on 26 September. Includes clearing **ash and
  debris from the Great Burn**, dismantling two climbable structures, the
  stage, and lake/pool equipment. Handover deadline **OPEN**.
- Rehearsal/soundcheck window: **OPEN**.

## 6. Permits, licences and compliance — assign responsibility explicitly

Nothing here is confirmed. For each, the contract should name who obtains
and who pays:

- Event/assembly permit and any local authority approvals.
- **Fire authority clearance** — required for the Great Burn, fire
  performers and torches; the highest-consequence approval on the list.
- Noise permission for overnight amplified sound to 9:30 AM.
- Water/boat safety compliance; pool operation.
- Structural sign-off for the 40 ft stage and both climbable installations.
- Food-service and hygiene approvals for the Feast Quarter.
- **Alcohol licence — not required. The event is alcohol-free (owner
  decision, 13 Aug 2026); see §8.** If a candidate venue mandates in-house
  bar service or a minimum bar spend, that conflicts with the event's own
  policy and must be negotiated out.
- **Drone permission: not secured**, airspace and venue rules unresolved —
  relevant because a videography quotation is under evaluation.
- Liability insurance (fire, working at height, water, 800+ overnight
  guests), and whether the venue requires being named as additional insured.

## 7. Commercial terms to settle

All **OPEN** — no venue figure exists anywhere in the project:

- Rent, deposit, and payment schedule; what triggers each tranche.
- **Cancellation and force majeure** — note the festival's published
  attendee policy: if the event is cancelled for reasons outside the
  organiser's control, **tickets carry over to the next edition** rather
  than being refunded. A venue cancellation clause that leaves the
  organiser holding both a lost deposit and a deferred-ticket liability
  should be negotiated hard.
- **Weather**: the event proceeds in light rain by published policy — the
  contract must not let the venue cancel for ordinary September rain.
  Define the severe-weather threshold and who calls it.
- Overtime/overstay charges past the strike deadline.
- Damage deposit and definition of fair wear — with fire, boats, climbing
  structures and 800+ overnight guests, expect scrutiny here; agree a
  documented pre-event site condition survey.
- **F&B exclusivity** — many venues mandate in-house catering or take a
  concession cut. Sonic Pulse brings its own **Feast Quarter vendors** and
  a bazaar; the published attendee policy bans outside food and drink and
  glass bottles. Any venue F&B monopoly directly conflicts with the
  designed programme.
- Utilities metering, cleaning charges, staff access, and whether the venue
  imposes preferred/mandated suppliers (which could collide with the
  already-contracted installation vendor and its exclusivity terms).
- **Confidentiality**: the venue address is deliberately unpublished — the
  site tells attendees the full address is emailed closer to the event.
  Agree announcement timing and any venue publicity requirements.

## 8. Alcohol — settled: the event is alcohol-free

The published site previously contradicted itself (the FAQ promised "a
fully stocked bar" while the policy page banned alcohol outright). **Owner
decision, 13 Aug 2026: there is no bar. No alcohol, narcotics or illegal
substances are permitted anywhere on the premises.** The website copy is
being corrected accordingly (REDESIGN_PLAN.md §8.25).

Contract consequences: no alcohol licence, no licensed-premises
obligations, and no bar-service or minimum-bar-spend clause. Security
scope is enforcement — gate checks, refusing entry to and removing anyone
intoxicated — rather than licensed service supervision. Food and
non-alcoholic drink service (the Feast Quarter: street food, late-night
biryani, a vegetarian line, iced and hot chai) runs all night and is
supplied by the organiser's own vendors, which matters against any venue
F&B exclusivity demand (§7).

## 9. Working rules for the contracts session

- Never invent a number, date or site fact marked OPEN — list it as a
  question for the owner.
- Bangladeshi law governs; the organiser is Dhaka Music Festival. Multi-year
  or restraint-style clauses face enforceability limits and should be
  drafted narrowly (the same caution applied to the installation vendor's
  exclusivity term).
- The event is a **single night** — any clause assuming a multi-day hire or
  a "day 2" is an error.
- Cross-check: the installation vendor's 20 Sep delivery obligation, the
  organiser's material-purchasing responsibility under that contract, and
  this venue's access window must be mutually consistent.
- When a term changes money, tell the owner so the finance session can
  record it; when it changes dates, so the planner session can reschedule.
